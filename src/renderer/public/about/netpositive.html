The browser from the operating system that almost was

BeOS never got its chance, but it got its own browser — small, quick, and hung from a yellow tab instead of a title bar.

About this recreation

BeOS had no title bar at all. A tab sits on the window's top edge, only as wide as the window title needs, with the close box at one end and the zoom widget at the other — and beside it, nothing. Reframe reproduces that literally: the tab is the three window controls laid out in a line, so it grows and shrinks with the page title, and the window is told to leave its backing clear so the desktop really does show through next to it.

The six toolbar icons and the globe are the original bitmaps at 1:1. The tab, the hard single-pixel bevels and the hairlines are CSS, so the window stays resizable.

Period details

  • The yellow tab — #ffcb00 with a lit top edge and a brown foot, bold black title, no minimise widget because BeOS had none.
  • One flat row — no button chrome at all: the globe, the address field and six 28×24 icons sit straight on the #d8d8d8 face.
  • Address field on the left — NetPositive put it ahead of the buttons, the reverse of almost everything else, and so does this.
  • Hard edges throughout — one-pixel highlight top-left, one-pixel shadow bottom-right, never a blur.
  • Rocket for Home — the icon that took you back to the start page.

A note on the home page

The original opened netpositive:Startup.html, a local page that no longer exists anywhere. This theme points Home at the Haiku project instead — the open-source successor that kept BeOS going after Be Inc. was gone.
