Internet Explorer 5.1
for Mac OS X

Developer Preview 4 · 2000
The first browser to wear Aqua

Mac OS X was still a developer preview, Aqua was brand new, and this was the browser you saw it in first — pinstripes, a candy-striped title bar and a teal address band, months before most people had ever run the system.

About this recreation

The toolbar buttons, the spinning mark and the small @ bullet are the original bitmaps, placed at 1:1 and never rescaled. Everything around them — the pinstripes, the teal band, the window frame — is CSS, so the window stays resizable instead of being one fixed 860×671 screenshot. The page inside it is the live modern web.

The traffic lights are measured row by row off Mac OS X 10.0's own pixels: near-black at the top of the rim, a white gloss across the top third, the colour deep beneath it and glowing back up from the foot. Safari 1.0 and Netscape 7.02 in Reframe draw the very same gems.

Period details

  • Aqua pinstripes — the four-row cycle #ffffff #eeeeee #e6e6e6 #eeeeee under a 10.0 title bar.
  • Labelled toolbar cells — 56×40 each, the label baked into the artwork. Back, Forward, Stop, Refresh and Print dim when there is nothing to do; Home and Mail are always live; AutoFill was greyed out in the original and stays that way here.
  • The teal address band — white “Address :” on the gradient, a sunken field with the @ badge, and the small ›go at its right.
  • Favorites bar — @-bulleted links in Geneva, the row Apple shipped on a fresh install.
  • The spinning mark — 57 frames, about three seconds a turn, measured against a recording of the real thing rather than guessed.

Not included

The Explorer Bar — the side panel with Favorites, History, Search, Scrapbook and Page Holder — is missing. Reframe's themes are pure data (a manifest, a stylesheet and assets), and a docked panel needs application code rather than CSS. The tab strip that opened it is not drawn either, rather than leave a control that does nothing.
